One accounting, in GPU-hours

One accounting, in GPU-hours

One accounting

Batch is counted in GPU-equivalent hours (sreport, after TRESBillingWeights). Inference is counted in tokens, per team (the gateway’s spend logs). They are reconciled as follows:

  1. Over the period (a month), read the GPU-hours of the svc_llm account: H_svc.
  2. Read each team’s spend in the gateway: D_A, D_B.
  3. Charge H_svc x D_A / (D_A + D_B) to A, and the remainder to B.
  4. Compare each institution’s total consumption (batch plus charged inference) with its share (81.25 % / 18.75 %).

Calibrating the internal cost

Goal: 1 unit of gateway spend = 1 GPU-hour. A saturated 8-GPU instance then “spends” 8 units per hour, and max_budget becomes a quota of inference GPU-hours, directly comparable with batch.

1. Measure the capacity. Once per model and per configuration (TP, KV-cache dtype), with vllm bench serve [24]. Use a mix of lengths representative of the real usage (for example 2,000 input tokens and 300 output tokens for a code assistant). Raise --max-concurrency up to the limit of the service target, for instance a p99 time to first token under 2 s:

vllm bench serve --backend openai-chat --endpoint /v1/chat/completions \
  --host gpu01.internal --port 8000 --model <model> \
  --dataset-name random --random-input-len 2000 --random-output-len 300 \
  --num-prompts 2000 --max-concurrency 64 --save-result

At the chosen concurrency C (which also serves the fair-share relay), read the output throughput T_out and the total throughput T_tot in tokens/s, printed by the tool [24]. The input throughput is T_in = T_tot - T_out.

2. Split the cost of an instance-hour. Prefill (input) costs much less per token than generation. Fix a ratio r = c_out / c_in, typically r = 4, and solve:

3600 (c_in T_in + c_out T_out) = 8
  =>  c_in = 8 / (3600 (T_in + r T_out)),   c_out = r c_in

A fictitious example, to illustrate the computation (the real figures come out of step 1): T_in = 40,000, T_out = 6,000 tokens/s, r = 4. Then c_in = 8 / (3600 x 64,000) = 3.5e-8 and c_out = 1.4e-7 unit per token.

3. Tokens served from the prefix cache. LiteLLM warns that a model with custom pricing bills these tokens at 0 as long as cache_read_input_token_cost is not set (a warning observed at LiteLLM 1.106.2 start-up). Set it to about c_in / 10: these tokens are not recomputed, but they occupy KV memory.

Why not a cost per second? LiteLLM accepts cost_per_second [22]. But vLLM processes dozens of requests simultaneously on one instance: billing each by its duration would make it pay for the whole instance, and the sum would far exceed the real 8 GPU-hours. The per-token cost is the right model.

Recalibrate at each change of model, vLLM version or KV-cache dtype, and check once a month. The GPU-hours of the svc_llm account read from sreport should stay close to the month’s total gateway spend divided by the average occupancy.

Compensation

If, over a quarter, an institution consumed markedly more than its share through inference, the committee may correct the Slurm Fairshare values of the next quarter. Batch fair-share already rebalances by itself within the half-life window: compensation is only an exceptional adjustment.
