Fair-share on a co-owned GPU cluster

Fair-share on a co-owned GPU cluster

How to share four HGX B300 servers (32 GPUs) between two institutions, A and B, with Slurm for batch computing, vLLM for inference, a gateway for keys and quotas, and VS Code to reach it all. The sharing must respect each institution’s share and leave no GPU idle.

This is an English adaptation of a design and operations document, version 1.1 of 23 October 2026. Every parameter cited refers to the vendors’ documentation consulted on that date (see the bibliography). The measurements are real and dated: they were made on LiteLLM 1.106.2 on 23 October 2026.

The guide has three layers, one page each:

  1. Slurm guarantees each institution its share (QOS caps, preemption of borrowing jobs) and divides the rest by fair-share (the Fair Tree algorithm): Slurm.
  2. vLLM serves the language models in long Slurm jobs, and a gateway in front of it authenticates users, applies per-institution quotas and counts consumption in tokens: Inference.
  3. One accounting, in GPU-hours, ties the two: Accounting.

Then VS Code access, identity with your federation (RENATER, eduGAIN), the security audit, acceptance tests and governance, and the bibliography.

Context and goals

The hardware

Each server is an HGX B300 with 8 Blackwell Ultra SXM GPUs linked by NVLink 5. Figures are NVIDIA’s (the HGX datasheet, the Blackwell Ultra technical blog and the DGX B300 user guide).

Per-GPU and per-server figures are not always in a ratio of 8: 108/8 = 13.5 PFLOPS rather than 15, and 2.1 TB rather than 8 x 288 GB. NVIDIA states that memory and SM count vary by part number. Use the values of your vendor’s datasheet. As an example, the DGX B300 has 2 Xeon Platinum 6776P and 2 TB of RAM (up to 4).

The B300 is an AI machine, not a double-precision scientific one. 10 TFLOPS of FP64 for 8 GPUs, and an INT8 rate divided by 24 relative to the B200. A classic FP64 code (CFD, quantum chemistry, finite elements) wastes its institution’s share here. The agreement between the institutions should say so; such codes belong on other facilities.

The cluster has 32 GPUs. A practical consequence: a model served with tensor parallelism 8 (--tensor-parallel-size 8) occupies a whole server, because NVLink does not connect two servers.

Ownership

Institution A bought 3 whole servers and the remaining quarter of the 4th; institution B bought the other three quarters of the 4th. If a later purchase changes the split, update this table and the values derived from it (Fairshare, GrpTRES).

Slurm shares are expressed in GPUs, which keeps them readable. Slurm normalises against siblings (S = S_self / S_(self+siblings)), so only the ratio 26:6 counts.

Objectives

  1. Guarantee: each institution gets its share (26 or 6 GPUs) within a bounded, known delay, even when the cluster is full.
  2. No loss: a GPU its owner does not use is lent to the other institution.
  3. Fairness over time: over a sliding window, each one’s consumption tends to its share. Whoever borrowed a lot goes behind the other.
  4. One accounting: batch and inference are both read in GPU-hours, for re-invoicing and governance.
  5. Security: GPU isolation between jobs, and no inference service reachable without authentication.

Design choices

Three possible models

ModelAdvantagesDrawbacks
Fixed partitionssimple; immediate guaranteeGPUs lost when the owner is idle; B can never run a TP=8 job (8 GPUs)
Pure fair-shareno GPU lost; fairness in the long runno guarantee on the delay: B may wait for A’s long jobs to end
Hybrid (chosen)bounded guarantee, lending of surplus, fairnesspreemption: borrowed jobs must be able to resume (checkpoint)

The hybrid model in one sentence

By default each institution submits to its own owner QOS, capped at its share; those jobs are never preempted. Beyond its share it submits to the borrow QOS, uncapped but preemptible by the owner QOS. Among waiting jobs, the order depends on fair-share, which penalises the institution that consumed more than its share over the last two weeks.
