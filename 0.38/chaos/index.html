Chaos

Chaos: faults in, invariants out

llmchaos (in bench, cmd/llmchaos) attacks a llmflow-gateway binary from the outside. It writes the configuration of a multi-institution deployment, starts the gateway as a process, injects faults while clients send traffic, and judges nine invariants after every scenario. A scenario is a row of a Go table and is replayed from its name and a seed.

go install github.com/go-llmflow/bench/cmd/llmchaos@latest
llmchaos list
llmchaos run baseline upstream-cut-sse --seed 1 --seed 2 \
  --gateway ./llmflow-gateway --workdir ~/chaos-runs

The deployment

Three owner organisations with shares 50/30/20 of one pool of 8 slots; an ILaaS third party (tiers, share 0, borrow = "spare", a 15 % markup) reached through a federation mount; two identity providers (one with a legacy domain, one with domains) and a federation issuer accepting a list of schac_home_organization; hard and soft budgets on a key, a team, two organisations and a person; fluids with idle energy, fixed costs, a charge = "non_shareholders" credit and a time-of-use tariff; a fake DCGM exporter and a BMC over TLS (go-redfish mockbmc for the Redfish service, plus a controllable EnvironmentMetrics counter); two HA replicas on one PostgreSQL in CI. The upstream is the recording fake vLLM of bench behind a fault injector.

The invariants

#invarianthow it is judged
1no hard budget exceeded beyond the documented worst case, in every scopethe clients’ own sum of answers read in full against the limit, plus the gateway’s committed and held; the excess allowed is what answers cost above their W
2no request billed to another person or orgper key and per org, billed requests and cost must lie between what the caller received in full and what it could have been charged; a failed request bills nothing
3the spare borrower never goes over the owners; the owners’ share convergesborrower concurrency at the upstream never above the pool; X-Llmflow-Priority of the borrower behind every owner’s; after the owners ramp up (3 s grace), the borrower gets at most 10 % of the answers and each owner’s share of completed work is within 15 points of 50/30/20. Judged only in a scenario whose upstream models vLLM’s priority scheduling (a waiting queue ordered by priority then arrival, a waiting request never preempting a running one); elsewhere the shares are not judged and the verdict says so
4fluids conservationactive + idle = kWh per row; rows = GPU energy + stated overhead; fixed costs minus credits = the racks, credits = the depreciation when a non-shareholder used energy
5a closed month never changesPostgreSQL scenarios only (CI): the month that has just ended is closed during traffic; its fluids and spend reports must be byte-identical afterwards and a second close must answer 409. The closed month holds no traffic of the campaign (the gateway’s clock cannot be moved from outside), so this judges immutability of the snapshot, not the figures in it. Everywhere else: N/A
6refusals429 has a positive Retry-After; no 200 with an empty body; a token the gateway must refuse (clock skew beyond clock_skew, key removed from the JWKS) gets 401
7no leakthe harness’s goroutines and descriptors, and the gateway’s descriptors, return to their start
8HA spendspend neither lost nor doubled across replica kills, partitions and reloads
9federation metricsno other organisation or key in the exposition; the +Inf bucket equals the count of answered and refused requests; refusals are in +Inf only

Faults

Upstream: 5xx, hang, slow tokens, a stream cut mid-way, wrong or missing usage, /v1/models down at start, preemption bursts. Clients: disconnects at every stream phase, retry storms, n and best_of, bursts at the edge of a budget. Identity: JWKS down, rotated, clocks four minutes ahead or seven behind. BMC: offline, counter reset, power only. HA: a killed replica, a partition between replicas, a rolling reload. PostgreSQL: a restart and slow queries, through a relay (there is no Docker on the workstation). Faults combine in one scenario.

Reading a failure

A run leaves <workdir>/<scenario>/evidence: the clients’ ledger, the upstream’s witness of every hit, the spend, budget and fluids reports at the end, and the gateway log. A broken invariant is reduced to a deterministic test in the gateway before anything is claimed: first say whether the failure value is wrong or the check is. Several early failures of the campaign were the check’s: a Retry-After capped at an hour when a budget refusal rightly names the time to the period’s end; a power-only BMC the fake made inconsistent; a client that cancelled while the answer arrived; a fake upstream that ignored vLLM’s priority and so served the owners equally; and a token “signed with a removed key” answered 200, which was the verifier’s documented refetch bound (min_refresh, default one minute: until the key set is refetched, a removed key still verifies what it signed, and tokens of the new key are refused). With min_refresh = "5s" a deterministic test, at the library level and against the gateway binary, shows the removed key refused after the refetch.

The campaign in CI

The repository’s CI runs all 31 scenarios with seeds 1 to 5 against gateway v0.37.1 and v0.38.0, as six shards per release on GitHub runners (each with its own PostgreSQL 17), then merges the shards and fails on a failed invariant, a run that broke, was skipped or sent nothing, an N/A a scenario is not entitled to, and any scenario and seed that no shard ran. llmchaos diff finds no verdict that differs between the two releases. That is a statement about what the scenarios exercise: the v0.38.0 fixes that no scenario reaches (an organisation administrator creating users, the spare check before the guards, the saturation fallback’s spare check, n and best_of in fair share) cannot show in it. The one fix a scenario does reach is the name scope of a federation token: v0.38.0 refuses a federation token whose name is unscoped (401), which v0.37.1 accepted, and the first campaign showed it as 15 differing verdicts until the harness minted a scoped name (carol@univ-c.example) as the release notes require.

Not a gateway verdict, the first runs also found three defects of the harness: a fault lifted twice (a replica started a second time, the first process left running), the request count of a client that left before the upstream saw its request, and a port taken between its choice and the gateway’s bind. All three are fixed and have tests; an unreproduced failure of ha-kill-during-pg-restart (seed 2, v0.38.0: four answered requests not billed) happened while those stray replicas shared the database, and did not recur in ten later runs.

Behaviours the campaign confirmed

These are documented gateway behaviours that the invariants account for, and the verdicts name them when they occur.

  • A replica that cannot reach the pool owner admits at the lowest priority band (ha/node.go, lowest()), the band the spare borrower always takes. An owner’s request can therefore tie with the borrower’s while ownership changes (killed replica, reload, slow PostgreSQL). Invariant 3 counts these requests and does not judge them.
  • A killed replica loses at most the last second of its accounting queue (gateway README, durable accounting). The answers a replica served in the 2 seconds before a SIGKILL may go unbilled; invariants 2 and 8 allow exactly those, and nothing else.
  • A stream cut after its 200 ends as a clean EOF, with no error event and no [DONE]; the client keeps what it received. Reported as an observation under invariant 6.
  • A request that failed upstream is counted in requests with zero tokens and zero cost.
  • An upstream’s own 503 is passed through without Retry-After.

Key rotation: an exposure to know

The gateway refetches an issuer’s key set only when a token its held keys cannot verify arrives, and at most once per min_refresh (default 1 minute, minimum 5 seconds; jwt and sso blocks). A key removed from the issuer’s JWKS is therefore still accepted for the tokens it signed until the next refetch, and tokens of the new key are refused (401) in the meantime. llmchaos showed it (scenario idp-jwks-rotated) and two deterministic tests confirm that the removed key is refused once the refetch has happened. An operator handling a compromised signing key should restart the gateway or lower min_refresh (to 5 s) before rotating, and treat tokens already signed as valid until they expire or the refetch happens.

What it does not exercise

What invariant 5 judges is immutability only: the figures of a closed month (the gateway’s clock cannot be moved from outside, so the closed month is empty, and a month that holds the campaign’s traffic cannot be closed), soft-budget alert delivery, the gateway’s goroutines (no pprof is exposed), and a PostgreSQL restart by the server itself (the relay resets connections). The PostgreSQL and HA scenarios, migrate-users and the month close run only in the repository’s CI, against both pinned gateway releases, and fail the job when an invariant breaks, when a scenario sent nothing, or when one was skipped.
